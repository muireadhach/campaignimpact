GREGORY'S BROTHER GETS TICKET PLACE Kentucky Representative's Death Won't Affect Democrats' Standing: in House. BY HARRY BLOOM. Special Correspondent of The Star. LOUISVILLE. Ky.. October 17 The death of W. Voris Gregory. 58, for 10 years Representative from the first district of Kentucky, necessitat ing selection of another congressional nominee by the Democratic party, was an unexpected political event of the week in Kentucky. A meeting of the District Committee to pick a suc cessor was held in Mayfield, home of Representative Gregory. His brother, J. Noble Gregory, was tipped for the place, although there were half a dozen other contenders. Mr. Gregory, who was running for his sixth term, took ill during the Summer after he had won renomina tion by an overwhelming majority. Since Mr. Gregory ran in a district regarded as a Democratic Gibraltar, his death was not expected to affect the political complexion of the Ken tucky delegation. It is again expected to consist of eight Democrats and a Republican. The week's big meeting was a train shed rally for Col. Frank Knox. Senator Barkley, whose oratory has been utilized by the Democratic Na tional Committee in States where the outcome was regarded as close, has been assigned to close the campaign in his home State. He is booked lor nine speeches, one in each congres sional district. Gov. Bibb Graves of Alabama was another major speaking luminary who invaded the State this week in behalf of President Roosevelt. Gov. A. B. "Happy" Chandler has been touring Ohio and Missouri. He told an audience at Fulton, Mo., that the Democratic majority in Kentucky would reach 200,000 and expressed the opinion that hotly-contested Ohio ia now "safely Democratic." KNOX TAKES ROOSEVELT NORTH CAROLINA ROUTE Two Major Addresses Delivered by G. 0. P. Nominee at Chapel Hill and Asheville. BY ROBERT E. WILLIAMS, Special Correspondent of The Star. RALEIGH, N. C.. October 17 --Col. Frank Nnox, Republican vice presi dential candidate, came to North Carolina this week. He found the State safely Democratic and left It in the same condition. Col. Knox made two set speeches in the State, one at Chapel Hill, the seat of the State University, and the other at Asheville, with six rear plat form speeches at train stops in be tween the two main speeches. His course duplicated to a large extent the route followed by Presi dent Roosevelt in a "non-political" visit to the State. Both spoke at Asheville and both made identical train stops for short speeches at Greensboro and Salisbury. The Knox crowds at these three points were only fractions of the crowds which greeted President Roosevelt a month ago. From the standpoint of enthusiasm, the con trast was even greater.
