By WILLIAM K. HUTCHINSON ABOARD LANDON SUN FLOWER SPECIAL, EN ROUTE TO OKLAHOMA CITY, Oct. 22 (I.N.S.).--Appealing again for non partisan support of his presiden tial campaign. Governor Alfred M. Landon today fired another broad side at the New Deal, charging its underlying motive was to regi ment the individual and curb his liberties. The Republican nominee was speeding Eastward along the old Santa Fe Trail en route to close his campaign in the East. After speaking last night in Phoenix, Ariz.. his next address was sc'.ied uled in Oklahoma City tomorrow morning. The Governor spent hours in nis private car working on the major addresses he will deliver in Indian apolis Philadelphia, Pittsburgh, New York city and St. Louis. Only one subject was revealed --a de nunciation of the Roosevelt ad ministration for an "influx of spoilsmen into the Government service" and its alleged failure to maintain the merit system among Federal employes. Plans "Surprise" Move Landon apparently has decided to surprise his political opponents with the topics of his closing speeches. This would be in line with the surprise announcement of his "victory dash" to Maine earlier in the campaign and the suddenness of the announcement of the present transcontinental I tour after a dash to California. The Governor's train crossed into New Mexico from Arizona early this morning. At Phoenix he assailed the Ad ministration for attempting to regiment the American people in violation of their constitutional liberties. Landon said: "Today the people of this country have a choice between the American system and an opposite system. This will de termine whether they and their children will remain a free people, capable of self-govern ment, or be dominated by an all-powerful central authority. LIBERTY AT STAKE "There can be no question as to the road down which we are being led. Regimentation of the individual and curb of his lib erties underlie every one of the New Deal's plans. "This threatened change in our form of rovernment is not remote. Its effects will strike di rectly into the homes, jobs, and lives of every one of us. The reality of it is but too well known to us." Landon pointed out New Deal laws already had told farmers "what they could plant in their fields" and told workers "how many hours they could stay at their tasks." He added the "pres ent Administration of the grazing act is another example of Federal bureaucracy run wild." Warns Farmers, Labor He warned that if such regula tions were established "by one means or another," the govern ment would have equal power to tell a farmer to "cease farming entirely and start digging ditches" or to tell working men "to work twice their present hours at half their present pay." For Guidance Catholics throughout the world are looking to Catholics of Amer ica as though to a lighthouse guiding other nations out of op pression, His Eminence Eugenio Cardinal Pacelli, Secretary of State of the Vatican, today told more than 4,000 students and clergy at the Catholic University. The distinguished prelate de clared that under "the difficulties of the moment," the Pope looks to "his children" on this side of the ocean to further the cause of Catholicism throughout the world. High in his praise of the Cath olic University, Cardinal Pacelli stated that he has a special inter est and love for the local insti tution. He said that at one time he was to have joined the Catholic University staff. Exhorts Students The cardinal pointed out that within a year the university would celebrate the golden jubilee of its founding, and exhorted the stu dents to maintain "fidelity to the heritage of the past" as well as ad vancing in the stride of progress. He sketched the development of the university from one gradu ate school in theology to its present group of colleges in art, religion, and c cience. He said: "The university here has brought honor to the Catholics of America." Cardinal Pacelli advised the stu dents to study both science and religion and to discover the har mony that exists between the supernatural. In this way, he said, they would find peace and stability. Above all. he advised, Catholic youth should seek to spread their learning and make it available for the good of mankind. Liberty and civic equ 'y, he said, was one of the glc es of America. Gives Blessing Tire Cardinal, accompanied by the staff of the Apostolic delega tion, was introduced by the Most Rev. Michael J. Curley, Arch bishop of Baltimore and chancel i lor of Catholic University. After the ceremony, the Cardi nal blessed the students and granted them a holiday from classes. As he left the gymnasium, the path was lined with devotees who sought his blessing and to kiss the ring which signifies his Papal authority. Accompanied by the apostolic delegation, he proceeded to the Shrine of the Immaculate Con- I ception and there knelt in prayer. It was stressed by members of the apostolic delegation that the prelate will not discuss any dip lomatic or political issues with diplomatic representatives of this country.
