OVERWHELMING LANDON VICTORY SHOWN IN DIGEST POLL 370 ELECTORS FOR HIM, 161 TO ROOSEVELT Kansan Given 1,293,- 669, Against 972,897 Straw Votes By EARL GODWIN Governor Landon elected with 370 electoral votes to Roosevelt's 161. This will be the trend of elec tion headlines if the Nation's voters line up as indicated by the final report of the Literary Digest Poll to be published tomorrow. In the final count Landon re ceives 1,293,669 straw votes to Roosevelt's 972,897 and Lemke's 83.610. The Digest Poll shows Roosevelt slipping slightly since the publi cation of its count two weeks ago when it gave Roosevelt 161 votes. Gets "Solid South" The States carried by Roosevelt in the Digest Poll are Alabama, Arkansas, Florida, Georgia, Ken tucky, Louisiana, Maryland, Mis sissippi, New Mexico, North Caro lina, Oklahoma, South Carolina, Tennessee, Texas, Utah and Vir ginia. This nothing more than the "Solid South'' plus Utah and Oklahoma and the border States of Kentucky and Maryland thrown in. The Digest kills all Roosevelt hopes for the big pivotal States and kills these hopes with tremen dous percentages against the New Deal, such as Illinois, where the poll is a 5-to-4 count for Landon; Indiana, nearly 2 to 1 for Landon; Massachusetts, better than 3 to 1; Michigan, 2 to 1. New York State is given to Landon in a count show ing 162,260 straws for Landon and 139,277 for Roosevelt. Pennsyl vania, which the Roosevelt forces are claiming even now, is given to Landon by a 5-to-4 vote. In the face of a wide variety of other results from other polls, friends of the Digest poll point out that in 1924 it was 99.44 per cent correct. Forecast Smith Loss In 1928 it was approximately 96 per cent correct. It certainly fore cast the Solid South's split against Alfred E. Smith, and it forecast the failure of Smith to carry his own State of New York. In 1932 the Digest forecast 474 electoral votes for Roosevelt to Hoover's 57. This was close to the actual vote, which turned out to be Roosevelt 472, Hoover 59.' In fore casting the popular vote on the repeal of prohibition the Digest poll was off by only 6-100 of 1 per cent. This year other poll takers and many partisans declare the Digest, poll is wrong; that the result should be almost entirely re versed with President Roosevelt leading Landon with even a greater lead of States than Lan don is credited with by the Di gest. The Digest statement an nouncing the final result, says: "Never before have we re ceived so many different varie ties of criticism." The Digest goes on to repeat what it said in a previous year-- that it makes no claim to infal libility and did not invent the phrase "uncanny accuracy" so frequently applied to the Digest polls. Changes Possible The Digest speaks of lastmin ute switches in previous cam paigns, and there is certainly every possibility of some serious last-minute switches in this cam paign. The Democrats are meet ing serious trouble with the "pay envelope" propaganda which the President attacked at Wilkes- Barre. Pa., yesterday. At the same time the Digest statement makes an intersting confession: "We say now about Rhode Island and Massachusetts that our figures indicate in our own judgment too large a percent age for Mr. Landon." However, the poll directors do not believe Massachusetts or Rhode Island will be found in the Roosevelt column. They seem to think that not enough Demo cratic straws have come back to them from the large number mailed out. While the Roosevelt backers claim Pennsylvania by a series of calculations involving every county, the Digest poll declares it is not only safe for Landon but that there has been a reversal of senti ment against Roosevelt to a re markable extent in many Key stone State cities. Landon Given Coast The Pacific Coast is given to Roosevelt by the political writers, but not the Digest poll. The poll says: "A State like California is al ways a difficult State to get an accurate opinion from by the polling method, and we may be far astray." However, the Digest poll pre dicted that Upton Sinclair could not be elected governor in the face of political experts to the contrary. The Digest close, its statement with these words: "The poll represents the most extensive straw ballot in the field--the most experienced in view of its 25 years of perfecting --the most unbiased in view of its prestige--a poll that has al ways previously been correct." J., Oct. 30 (I.N.S.) --Crews of laborers worked until dawn today to clear the Penn sylvania Railroad tracks of the wreck of the crack flyer Cleve lander, which jumped the rails and left one man dead and at least 20 injured. It was feared for a time that Gov. Alfred M. Landon's "Sun flower Special," en route from New York to Charleston, W. Va., would have to be sent over an other route because of the wreex age on the main line, but one track was cleared so that the special passed the wreck at 2:12 a. m. The dead man, who was decapi tated, was believed to be Edward Snyder of Detroit. His clothing bore that name and he had a receipt in his pocket from the Central Radio Store of Detroit. The train was about a quarter of a mile out of Princeton Junc tion, when the last six cars left the tracks. Railroad officials today were in vestigating the possibility that the train may have lost a wheel, thus causing the swinging of one car to carry others from the rails. Hurtling cars smashed down several iron poles, supporting power lines, and rescuers had to work in the darkness.
