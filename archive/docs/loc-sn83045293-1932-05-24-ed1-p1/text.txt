Both wet and dry leaders in the house predicted today that at least 50 congressmen will be defeated be cause of their votes on beer. Representative O'Connor of New York, author of the defeated 2.75 per cent beer bill, declared the new house to be elected in November would show a majority for modifi cation. The beer bill was rejected by 228 to 169, so a shift of 30 votes would have given the anti-prohibi tionists a majority. The roll call vote put nearly every member on record publicly and wet organiza* tions will concentrate their cam paigns accordingly. Drys acknowledged they are in for a fight, but insisted that some of those who favored beer will find that they misjudged their constitu ents' views. Prohibitionists also pointed out that while the house majority against beer was only 59, recent votes in the senate show that body still dry by about three to one. Anti-prohibitionists were encour aged by three other developments: Senator Johnson of California, long an ardent dry, followed the lead of his colleague, Senator Shortridge, and came out for a referendum. Senator Borah of Idaho, a leading dry, indicated he would not attend the Republican national convention. He had been counted on by prohibi tionists to lead the fight against a referendum declaration this year. Republican Leader Snell of the house, slated to be chairman of the Republican convention, predicted it would adopt a "liberal" prohibition Jplank.
