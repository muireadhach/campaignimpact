ROOSEVELT POLICY RUINED FARM MARKETS, SAYS LANDON PLEDGES NEW U.S. TRADE PROGRAM Kansan Also Promises Return to Senate of Treaty Powers (Text of Governor Landon's speech on Page* 6.) By WILLIAM K. HUTCHINSON MINNEAPOLIS, Minn., Sept. 25 (I.N.S.).--Pledging repeal of the present reciprocal trade program, with a return of treaty-making powers to the Senate and tariff making powers to Congress, Gov ernor Alfred M. Landon today car ried his presidential campaign into Wisconsin after charging the Roosevelt foreign trade policies had "sold the American farmer down the river.'' The Republican nominee left here this morning aboard his "Sun flower Special'' for a series of "rear porch" talks in cities of Central Wisconsin, while en route to Milwaukee for a major speech tomorrow night on social security. Landon planned an attack on the Administration's law, which taxes both employers and employes to provide unemployment insurance, while offering his own substitute for the existing act. The Governor will turn aside from his campaign role tonight to become a football fan, while at tend a high school game at Osh kosh. He expressed a personal de sire to view the game, as he had no political appointment for the eve ning. Not "Reciprocity" In his second major speech here on the farm problem, Landon as sailed the Roosevelt reciprocal trade treaties as "destroying the markets of our farmers." He said the treaties, which admit huge quantities of farm products to compete against American farm products, did not constitute "recip rocity" but "charity paid for by American producers." The Governor's first farm speech at Des Moines Tuesday dealt with purely domestic issues. There he pledged an Administration that would "preserve the family type of farm" and "restore permanent agricultural prosperity." In his second address, he dealt with agri culture's foreign trade. The principle underlying present reciprocal treaties which did not constitute "reciprocity," were con demned as "injurious to American citizens." He declared himself "opposed to a policy of isolation," believing "reasonable protection is essential to our own well being." Landon said: "Eight of these agreements have been in force long enough for us to see the results. The im ports of farm products have in creased 84 per cent over a corre sponding previous period. "Exports of farm products have increased only 26 per cent In other words, under these eight agreements, our imports increased more than three times as fast as our exports." Landon condemned the entire principle of the present treaties. If they are commercial treaties, he said, the Senate ought to ratify them by a two-thirds vote. If they are tariff revisions, he added, Congress ought to enact them. He won tremendous applause for his farm program. Farm leaders declared he had presented a program that revealed his per sonal knowledge and experience of farming. Republicans told him his program insured a Republican vic tory throughout the farm belt. The governor's itinerary through Wisconsin called for stops at Eau Claire, Marshfield, Wausau, Green Bay, Appleton, Neenah Menasha, and Oshkosh at 8:20 p. m. Today's big event will be the night pageant and fireworks, the like of which, regatta officials say, has never before been seen in Washington. The parade, which will be headed by the District Fire Fighter with illuminated water display in full blast, will be com posed of more than 60 private and commercial craft brilliantly deco rated and illuminated. It will pass by the seawall and toward the Navy Yard, where the party will be officially greeted, and from there to the Corinthian Yacht Club for the now famous "Open House Party." The parade will have a queen-- Miss Leila Peoples, daughter of Admiral and Mrs. Christian J. Peoples, and she will be known as "Queen of the Night Pageant." She will have five princesses, all of whom will be escorted by young men selected from the District Na tional Guard, the District Naval Reserve and the Fleet Marine Re serve Corps. The fireworks will be set off from a barge anchored off Grav elly Point, quite a distance down the river so that its brilliance may be well seen from all points. During the entire pageant, the public address system will be in use to exolain the various tyoes of craft and their owners in th<* pa rade.
