The main points of the declara tion as finally adopted after amend ments is in part as follows: Considering the less advanced condi tion of American trade unions in mem bership, form of organisation, political activities and social concepts compared with the progressive organizations of the working class in other modern nations, and the lack of satisfactory understand ing between the trade unions and the Socialist party In matters of common concern to both, this convention affirms the need of educational work among trade unionists to effect fruitful co-oper ation between the Socialist party and the trade unions- Considering that technical improve ment and development of mass produc tion in all large industries are under mining old ttade skills and tending to reduce all wage workers to a common level of efficiency; that the working class suffers for lack of legal provisions protecting workers discarded at an early age; that millions of the unemployed and their families are dependent upon degrading charity doles because we have no system Of compulsory unemployment insurance; that "yellow dog'' contracts have received judicial sanction; that the legal status of trade unions is weak and precarious, and that effective working class political organization is urgently needed to cope with these problems, this convention declares: For Friendly Co-operation. Duty to Join Union. Since we believe that it is neces sary to build up a strong movement of the workers in the economic as well aS the political field and be cause a fully class-conscious mem bership and leadership is necessary to gain the fullest freedom for the workers, we declare it to be the duty of members of the Socialist party working in trades and industries where there are established unions to join such union and in trades and industries where there is no organization it is their duty to work for the establishment of unions." The first to pronounce, in glow ing words, his benediction upon the group, part of which had worked so hard to defeat him for national executive chairman the previous day, was Morris Hillquit, now launched upon his third term as titular head of the party. "It has been a very exciting con vention," he began, his voice a lit tle hoarse with the strain of the four-day session, "the most excit ing one I have attended since the Socialist party was formed. I am not discouraged by it. I am deeply encouraged. "The intensity of the interest which has been developed is the measure of your devotion. "Problems Handled With Fists." "We have felt that the cause is so important that we must give to it everything that is in us. "When we have clashed we have taken off our gloves and handled each problem with our naked hands." "At times," Hillquit smiled tol erantly, "we have handled them with our fists." Hillquit also added a final word of the philosophy for which he and those of the party leaders who agreed with him have stood throughout the past four years and the meeting just closed. Sees Huge Vote. "To roll up a big, impressive vote will be exceedingly easy this year," he said. "But this is not our principal ob jective. This is in a larger sense, our opportunity to rebuild the So cialist party on a permanent, last ing foundation." Tracing the history of the prog ress of the Socialist party in this country, Hillquit then said that there was a time when the party grew by leaps and bounds. From a membership of 15.000 in 1900, it grew in the next 15 years to a membership of 120,000, he pointed out. And from an early vote of 100,000 the party was able to count 1,000,000 ballots at its peak. "If we had maintained that rate of growth, we would now normally have a membership of 500,000 and be polling a vote of 5,000,000," he continued. "Party Stands United." "And we should have scores of flourishing Socialist daily newspa pers if the unfortunate war perse cutions had not checked our prog ress. But the war cloud is lifting now and we find ourselves on the eve of a great awakening. The new national executive committee will go into session tomorrow morning with all this in mind, to formulate immediate plans for the coming campaign."
